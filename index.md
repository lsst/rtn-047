# The Case for a Fully Public Rubin EDF-S Dataset


## Recommendation

Designate Rubin Observatory data in the Euclid Deep Field South (EDF-S) as fully world-public on an ongoing basis, establishing EDF-S as both a standing legacy field following the model of COSMOS, and providing the Rubin data necessary to produce an open, validated, multi-survey AI-ready dataset across the three current major large sky surveys to facilitate joint survey processing.

## Discussion

The Euclid Consortium and Rubin Observatory are jointly observing the 23 deg² Euclid Deep Field South (EDF-S) to a combined depth and cadence beyond either survey's individual wide-area program. A 2022 white paper ({cite}`Guy2022`) defined an initial set of Rubin-Euclid Derived Data Products (DDPs) specifically to realize the far greater science return of the two surveys combined. This vision has an obvious near-term extension to NASA's Roman Space Telescope. Yet different data access policies across these surveys are hampering efforts to realize those DDPs, blocking the combined analysis the DDP program was designed to enable.

This proposal therefore recommends that Rubin's EDF-S data be designated fully world-public, giving the community access to data from all three key surveys over one common field. What EDF-S provides, uniquely, is a small, non-proprietary testbed where the community can build and validate the joint services, pipelines, and analyses that cross-survey processing will require, before attempting it at full scale. Potential science applications include weak-lensing cosmology, photometry and redshifts for crowded or faint galaxies, AGN and black-hole studies, and cross-matched transient classification.

There is also a strong benefit for AI research. A validated dataset spanning Rubin, Euclid, and Roman over the same footprint would provide a high-value training and benchmarking resource that does not currently exist. A separate community proposal, "The Cross-Survey Decade: A Call to Action" ({cite}`Rau2026`), whose co-authors include several Rubin staff, identifies EDF-S as a candidate starting point for this kind of joint processing.

The risk to Rubin science is minimal: EDF-S covers only about 23 deg² of Rubin's approximately 18,000 deg² survey area and does not materially affect any proprietary program. The potential upsides, by contrast, are considerable. It gives Rubin a field where it can openly showcase its groundbreaking and unique science to the broadest possible audience, a strong win for public and community engagement. Beyond that, open, non-proprietary fields have historically become the community's most productive resources: the COSMOS field remains a cornerstone of astrophysics decades after its inception, precisely because it was kept open from the start. The same pattern holds for datasets that have driven progress in AI: the Multimodal Universe dataset, ImageNet, and AlphaFold's structural archive were all released openly from the start, and each catalyzed a research community that built on it for years afterward. A fully open Rubin EDF-S has the potential to do the same for astrophysics and AI.
